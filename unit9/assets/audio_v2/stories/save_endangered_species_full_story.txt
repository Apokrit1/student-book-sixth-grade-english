At the coastal rescue station on the island of Zakynthos, volunteers work seven days a week to protect endangered species in Mediterranean waters. Ranger Elena explained that loggerhead sea turtles have nested here for thousands of years, but human noise and bright beach lights can disturb the mother turtles when they crawl ashore to lay eggs. If people damage these quiet nesting sands, this precious natural habitat could disappear forever. Last night, Elena guarded a mother turtle as she laid more than one hundred round eggs inside a deep sand hole. In the laboratory, veterinary doctors care for an injured turtle that had swallowed plastic bags; dirty marine waste can cause serious internal disease in young sea creatures. The rangers carefully weigh each newly hatched baby turtle before watching it head for the moonlit sea, hoping none of these magnificent animals will ever become extinct, thanks to active environmental education.
